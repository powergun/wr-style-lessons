## Summing Up

1. Readers judge prose to be clear when subjects of sentences name characters and verbs name actions.

2. If you tell a story in which you make abstract nominalizations the main characters and subjects, use as few other nominalizations as you can:

 $ \underline{\text{A NOMINALIZATION}} $ is a REPLACEMENT of a verb by a noun, often resulting in DISPLACEMENT of characters from subjects by nouns.

✓ When  $ \underline{\text{a NOMINALIZATION}} $ replaces a verb with a noun, it often displaces characters from subjects.

3. Use a passive verb if the agent of an action is self-evident and so can go unstated:

 $ \underline{\text{The voters}} $ reelected the president with 54% of the vote.

✓  $ \underline{\text{The president}} $ was reelected with 54% of the vote.

4. Use a passive verb if it lets you replace a long subject with a short one:

 $ \underline{\text{Research demonstrating the soundness of our reasoning and the need for action supported}} $ this decision.

✓  $ \underline{\text{This decision}} $ was supported by research demonstrating the soundness of our reasoning and the need for action.
