### Here's the Point

We depend on concepts running through a passage to create a sense of its coherence. You help readers identify those concepts in two ways:

- Repeat those that name characters as topics of sentences, usually as subjects.

• Repeat others as themes elsewhere in a passage, in nouns, verbs, and adjectives.

Readers are more likely to notice those themes if you emphasize them at the end of the sentence that introduces the passage.

### Quick Tip

For a paragraph more than five or six sentences long, underline the sentence that you think best introduces or frames the rest of the paragraph. If you can't do that quickly, the paragraph probably has a problem. If you can, circle the important words in the sentence. Those words should sound like a title for the paragraph. If they do not, your paragraph may confuse your readers. We will return to this matter in Lesson 7.
