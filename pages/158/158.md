Here, the predicate of a relative clause in a sentence's subject balances the predicate of the main clause:

 $$ \begin{aligned}&A\text{government}\left[\begin{aligned}\\ &that~is~unwilling~to~listen~to~the\\&moderate~hopes~of~its~citizenry\\&must~eventually~answer~to~the~harsh\\&justice~of~its~revolutionaries.\\ &\end{aligned}\right]\\ \end{aligned} $$ 

Here a direct object balances the object of a preposition:

Those of us concerned with our school systems will not sacrifice

[the intellectual growth of our innocent children to the social engineering of incompetent bureaucrats.]

In that sentence,

- a subordinate clause, 1a Were I trading..., balances the main clause, 1b I would not be writing...;

- the object of that subordinate clause, 2a scholarly principles, balances the object in the prepositional phrase, 2b financial security;

- the object in the main clause, 3a short books, balances objects in two prepositional phrases, 3b minor subjects and 3c small audiences (with the balanced short, minor, and small).

Remember that you usually create the most rhythmical balance when each succeeding balanced element is a bit longer than the previous one (see pp. 136–137).

These patterns encourage you to think in ways that you otherwise might not. In that sense, they don't just shape your thinking; they generate it. Suppose you begin a sentence like this:

In his earliest years, Picasso was a master draftsman of the traditional human form.
